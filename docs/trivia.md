# Trivia

Observations about the game that the disassembly makes checkable, but that are neither bugs nor unused content.

## The credit pictures show levels that aren't in the game

The ending starts when King K. Rool is beaten. Collecting the banana at his location ($3C) runs `BossDefeated_Banana`, which sets bit 4 of `wMapFlags`, and leaving the level then starts the flow script at `Flow_Ending` (bank `$0D`). The script plays song `$13` and shows 28 full-screen pictures, each for 360 frames, about six seconds. The first is the "Donkey Kong Country Game Boy Color Team" title card and the rest carry the staff names in groups. A "Thank you for playing" screen follows, built mostly from the title screen's logo tiles. Then comes an end message and finally "The End" (`Flow_TheEnd`).

Each picture behind the names is a staged scene that looks like a moment from a level: Kongs, animal buddies and enemies on jungle ground, among pine trees, on rope bridges, on mine tracks, on factory girders, by snowy mountains or under water. None of the scenes matches a place in the game's levels, and several put characters where they never appear in play. Fish swim through open air above jungle ground behind the last group of Game Boy Color team names (screen `$47:6446`), and a swordfish and two sharks float above a coral hill under a blue sky behind the NOE Germany names (`$4A:7872`).

The tile data shows how the pictures were made. `tools/credits_tiles.py` compares the tiles each picture uses with every level tileset, the sprite graphics, the title pictures and the other full screens, counting a tile as the same when it is only flipped. Between 8% and 58% of each picture's distinct non-blank tiles also occur in a level tileset. With `--mask` the tool dims every other tile, and the bright parts that remain are the scenery: ground, trees, palms, mountains, bridges, rails, girders, ice and coral, taken over from the levels' graphics. The characters, barrels, bananas and letters are not the game's sprites: at most 11 of a picture's tiles also occur in the sprite graphics. They are drawn as background tiles of their own, together with the lettering of the names and the tiles where a character overlaps the scenery. So each card is a collage: real level scenery, arranged freely, with the cast painted in.

The end message depends on the save file (`State_ChooseEndingMessage`). At exactly 101% (the total from `Save_CountCompletion`), the game shows Diddy over DK's banana hoard with no text (`Flow_EndingHoard`). Otherwise it shows the same picture with a hint in the language chosen at start-up, in English "Secrets await you on another barrel option" (`Flow_EndingHintEnglish` and the French, German, Italian and Spanish versions, listed in `EndingMessageFlows`). The hint probably points to the front end's extras that unlock with progress (see the README's front-end section); that reading is a guess.
